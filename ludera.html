Ludera Website Redesign

Overview

I was part of the Riipen April 2026 Cohort and worked for Ludera, an edtech, gaming startup developing a role-playing game (RPG) dedicated to redefining the landscape of learning. As part of their team, I was responsible for proposing and implementing a potential website redesign in Figma.

Below I detail my workflow, key findings, proposed solutions and key takeaways from the learning experience. For the sake of privacy, I will not be disclosing any Ludera-specific assets.

01 Website Audit

I audited Ludera's digital assets and website using two "lenses":

  • Design-Based Evaluation: Using graphic design and UI/UX princples to identify design inconsistencies and potential usability issues
  • Contextual Evaluation: Using/identifying the established marketing objectives, product positioning and target audience to determine whether the existing design and content support them

While the first focused on whether the design followed established principles, the second considered its appropriateness for Ludera's specific goals (hence the word "contextual"). However, they were still interrelated because design choices could be consistent with general principles yet still be ineffective in the context of an team's/company's objectives.

02 Key Pain Areas of Improvement
  1. Mixed Brand Messaging
    Ludera was positioned as a "game first," but its website relied on traditional edtech copy (ie. "study", "notes," "lecture slides"). This can emphasize educational workflows over gameplay, potentially making Ludera appear to be a study tool with gamification features.

  2. High-Density Content/Irrelevant Information for Dual Audiences
    Ludera had student-facing engagement content alongside investor-oriented information on its landing page. This created a dense landing page with competing communication priorities.
03 Proposed Solutions
  1. Reframe Learning as a Game Mechanic
    The desired positioning is to make Ludera to feel like a game where learning is intrinsic to its game mechanics. To have end users go
    • From: “I use Ludera because I need to (force myself to) study for a midterm”
    • To: “I want to see what happens next in Ludera, and learning is how I (happen to) progress”

    To do this, I suggested avoiding vocabulary/language that emphasize educational workflows unless absolutely necessary.

    I also suggested framing knowledge acquisition as a gameplay mechanic that unlocks progression, rewards, upgrades, rare loot, cosmetics, etc. One example is to go

  2. Allocate 80%-90% of the Landing Page to End-User-Facing Content
    The Home Page serves as the highest-visibility entry point for prospective users and should therefore focus on end user acquisition and engagement.

    The idea is that making end users hooked, or the attempt at doing so, may indirectly signal enough credibility for investors to want to back it. Otherwise, the bottom line is that the website can better serve its primary audience while allowing investors to explore the product on their own terms.

    To do this, I suggested dedicating the landing page to: immersion and worldbuilding, lore teasers, game features and visual demonstrations of the game experience. Then I suggested to have a navigation bar (header and/or footer) that instantly routes investors to pages dedicated to data-driven, metric-focused information.
